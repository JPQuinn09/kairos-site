What Kairos is

Kairos helps you plan, run, and record ERP exposures, and shows how your distress changes over time.

The exercises themselves are generic examples written in advance, not a plan prepared for you. Which of them the app shows you is based on your quiz answers, and some themes are withheld or carry a note advising specialist support. That is content selection, not a clinical judgement about you, and no part of it is a diagnosis.

The exercises and safety information were written by the developer from published research. They have not yet been reviewed by a qualified clinician. Kairos has not been reviewed or approved by any regulator. It is a tool for practising a method, not a substitute for assessment or treatment by a clinician.

Using it safely

When you first open Kairos you are shown a short screen setting out what it is, what it is not, and where to go in a crisis. You can read it again at any time from Profile.

  • Work at a level you can actually tolerate. Nothing in the app requires you to do any specific exercise.
  • Stop if an exposure feels unsafe, and speak to a professional.
  • Exposure exercises are meant to be uncomfortable and can make symptoms feel worse for a time. You choose whether to do any exercise, and you are responsible for doing it safely and lawfully.
  • Never do an exercise where it could put you or anyone else in danger.
  • Nothing in Kairos is a reason to delay or avoid seeing a doctor.
  • Do not use Kairos in place of urgent help. If you are in danger, contact emergency services — 999 in the UK, 911 in the US — or a helpline on the app's Crisis Support screen.
  • If you are under the care of a clinician, tell them you are using a self-guided tool.

Who can use Kairos

You must be 16 or over to use Kairos. The app asks you to confirm this, and to agree to your logs being stored in your account, before it can be used. If you are under 18, we suggest you involve a parent, carer or therapist. If you are under 16, please do not use Kairos: speak to your GP, or to a parent or carer, about getting support for OCD instead.
